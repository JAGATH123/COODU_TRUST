Understanding

What disease-specific interventions mean

What are disease-specific interventions?

Disease-specific interventions are targeted health programmes designed to address specific diseases, infections or health conditions through focused prevention, control and treatment strategies.

These interventions concentrate on particular conditions such as HIV/AIDS, COVID-19 and vector-borne diseases, with specialised approaches tailored to the unique characteristics, transmission patterns and risk factors of each — 5 target diseases addressed across our work.

Our programmes reach high-risk populations with evidence-based prevention, awareness campaigns and coordination with health authorities for effective disease control. Working with vulnerable communities, migrants, urban slum populations and other at-risk groups, we have reached 25,000+ people across 15 high-risk communities to prevent transmission and provide appropriate care and support.

  • Targeted prevention
  • Awareness & education
  • Screening & testing
  • Vector control
  • Community response

Our targeted intervention process

  1. Map & identify Community mapping and identification of vulnerable populations — migrants, urban slum residents and other at-risk groups.
  2. Raise awareness Disease-specific health education, awareness campaigns and behavioural change communication.
  3. Screen & detect early Regular screening programmes, testing camps and early detection initiatives for targeted diseases.
  4. Control & support Vector control, coordination with health authorities, and appropriate care and support for affected people.

Why this work matters

The challenge on the ground — and our answer

The challenge

HIV risk concentrates in overlooked groups

Migrants and female sex workers face the highest HIV/AIDS transmission risk — and are the least reached by mainstream health services.

Our response

Targeted intervention for high-risk groups

An ongoing HIV/AIDS prevention programme for migrants and female sex workers — awareness, counselling, STI camps and condom promotion.

The challenge

Epidemics hit crowded slums hardest

In dense urban slums, COVID-19 spread fast while the most vulnerable households stayed invisible to the health system.

Our response

Map, identify and coordinate

The COVID-19 community intervention programme mapped urban slums, identified vulnerable populations, ran awareness drives and coordinated with health authorities.

The challenge

Mosquitoes breed at the doorstep

Stagnant water in and around homes breeds the mosquitoes that carry dengue and malaria through whole neighbourhoods.

Our response

Find and eliminate breeding grounds

Domestic breeding checkers identify and eliminate mosquito breeding grounds house by house, preventing vector-borne disease before it starts.

The challenge

Low awareness lets preventable disease spread

Without disease-specific knowledge, at-risk communities miss the early signs and the simple behaviours that stop transmission.

Our response

Education, early detection, local control

Awareness campaigns, behavioural change communication and community-based disease control — an 80% prevention success rate across our targeted interventions.
