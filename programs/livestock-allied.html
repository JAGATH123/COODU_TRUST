Livestock & allied activities

Promoting livestock development and allied activities for diversified income sources across rural Tamil Nadu.

4,660+ Animals treated
7,500+ Farmers & women trained
1,202 Milch animals distributed
6+ Districts covered

Understanding

What livestock & allied activities mean

What are livestock & allied activities?

Livestock & allied activities encompass the breeding, rearing and management of domesticated animals — including dairy farming, animal husbandry, veterinary services and value-added processing.

Our approach focuses on improving animal productivity, enhancing veterinary care, establishing dairy enterprises and developing market linkages for livestock products — sustainable management practices that ensure animal welfare and economic viability, with 4,660+ animals treated and 1,202 milch animals distributed.

The work integrates quality breeding stock, modern animal husbandry techniques, feed production units and value chains for milk, eggs and meat products — giving 7,500+ trained farmers and women regular income and nutritional security.

  • Dairy development
  • Veterinary services
  • Feed & fodder
  • Value addition

Our livestock development process

  1. Animal selection Identifying quality breeding stock and livestock varieties suitable for local conditions.
  2. Husbandry training Training farmers in modern animal care, feeding, breeding and disease prevention.
  3. Health management Providing veterinary services, vaccination programs and health monitoring systems.
  4. Market integration Establishing processing units and market linkages for livestock products.

Why this work matters

The challenge on the ground — and our answer

The challenge

One crop, one fragile income

When a family depends on crop farming alone, a single failed season can take the whole year's income with it.

Our response

Daily income from livestock

Dairy, poultry, goat rearing and fisheries provide steady cash flow through daily milk sales, egg production and periodic sale of animals — 1,202 milch animals distributed.

The challenge

Disease drains a family's biggest asset

Without healthcare and vaccination, livestock — often a rural household's most valuable asset — is lost to preventable disease.

Our response

Veterinary care and vaccination

Veterinary services, vaccination programs, health monitoring and preventive treatment keep herds healthy — 4,660+ animals treated so far.

The challenge

Husbandry skills that limit productivity

Without training in modern animal care, feeding and breeding, milk yields and farmer incomes stay low.

Our response

Technical training at scale

7,500+ farmers and women trained in dairy farming, animal husbandry and livestock management to improve productivity and capability.

The challenge

Few income opportunities for rural women

Rural women often lack independent income sources of their own within farming households.

Our response

Dairy entrepreneurship for women

Through the RMK and Mahalir Thittam projects, women build dairy enterprises with entrepreneurship training, milk collection systems and market linkages.
