Understanding

What strategic planning & advisory means

What are strategic planning & advisory services?

Strategic planning & advisory services mean being engaged by a government department or agency to design, support or evaluate a programme rather than to run it. Coodu Trust has held a number of such assignments, and this page sets out what they were.

The trust has carried a Research & Consultancy division since at least 2005–06, and states as one of its objectives that it will “provide consultancy services in project design, management and evaluation of developmental projects”. “Research, monitoring, evaluation and documentation” is still listed as one of its core areas of work in the 2024–25 annual report.

The 2011–12 annual report sets out four client assignments in full, with values, durations and staff-months. The largest was the capacity building of Water Users Associations for Participatory Irrigation Management in the Arjuna nadhi, Nichaba nadhi and Kalingalar sub-basins — ₹1,11,30,000 over 30 months and 630 staff-months for the Executive Engineer, WRO/PWD, Upper Vaippar Basin, Rajapalayam. Alongside it ran an Integrated Watershed Management Programme assignment of ₹18,00,000 for the Joint Directors of Agriculture in Karur, Namakkal, Tirunelveli and Tiruvallur; a Mahalir Thittam assignment of ₹39,60,000 for the Project Officer in Karur; and a Targeted Intervention of ₹19,00,000 for TANSACS in Pudukottai.

Earlier and alongside those contracts, the trust's advisory work took the form of evaluation and survey assignments for District Rural Development Agencies — mid-term and final evaluations of watershed projects, performance reports, socio-economic and benchmark surveys, Below Poverty Line survey computerisation, a rapid livelihoods assessment of the Piranmalai Kallar communities across five blocks of Dindigul, and the re-survey of bonded labourers in Karur district for the Adi Dravidar Welfare Department.

Our consultancy approach

  1. Entry point activities and rapport building The first item on the trust's own list of services for the IWMP assignment — establishing a working relationship in the village before anything is planned.
  2. Participatory Rural Appraisal and action planning PRA in the micro-watershed area, followed by an action plan for implementation — transect walks, resource and social mapping, seasonality analysis, wealth and matrix ranking, timelines and SWOT.
  3. Capacity building and institution formation District-level training for watershed user groups and self-help groups, formation of women's groups in the watershed area, and awareness for women members of Water Users Associations.
  4. Evaluation and convergence Mid-term and impact evaluation, socio-economic and benchmark surveys, and convergence with all government line departments.

Why this work matters

The challenge on the ground — and our answer

The challenge

Program ideas that never become results

Development programs launched without rigorous design struggle to deliver measurable outcomes on the ground.

Our response

Plan from what the village tells you

Participatory Rural Appraisal in the micro-watershed area, then an action plan built on it — the method the trust used across 479 village panchayats and set out as its service list for the IWMP assignment.

The challenge

Advice detached from implementation

Pure consultancy firms recommend strategies they have never had to execute in real villages and towns.

Our response

We practice what we preach

Our advice comes from coordinating watershed programmes in 570 watersheds across 14 districts, 18,592 skill-training beneficiaries, and running the solid waste management programme for Dindigul City Municipal Corporation.

The challenge

Grassroots organizations with weak systems

Community-based organizations often lack the governance, financial systems and compliance frameworks to grow and last.

Our response

Institutional strengthening

Under the WRO/PWD consultancy the trust built the capacity of every member of the Water Users Association managing committees and of their four sub-committees, and worked specifically to bring women members into active roles.

The challenge

Programs that end when the consultant leaves

Without knowledge transfer and local capacity, gains fade as soon as external support is withdrawn.

Our response

Knowledge transfer & capacity building

Seven separate watershed training manuals were written and published for each block we worked in, alongside project reports, survey reports and IEC material — so the knowledge stayed with the community institutions after the assignment ended.
