Health support & rehabilitation

A short stay home, an old age home and a de-addiction centre — residential care for people the health system does not shelter.

510 Sheltered in 2020–21
89 Elders cared for, 2020–21
57 In the de-addiction home, 2022–23

Understanding

What health support & rehabilitation means

What is health support & rehabilitation?

Health support & rehabilitation means a bed, a meal and somebody to talk to — residential care for people living with a condition, an addiction or an old age that leaves them with nowhere else to go.

The work began in November 2007, when the trust opened a short stay home for people living with HIV/AIDS, with a drug de-addiction centre in the same building, at Mylampatti, Tharagampatti post, Karur district. It was set up at the insistence of the women’s Self-Help Groups the trust worked with, who had identified people living with HIV/AIDS among their own families and neighbours. The home had ten beds, a laboratory and a kitchen, separate wards for women and men, and a staff team of a part-time doctor, trained staff nurses, a counsellor, outreach workers, a lab technician, a cook and a watchman.

Today the trust runs a Short Stay Home and Old Age Home in Dindigul for elderly people and for those with no home of their own. In 2020–21 it sheltered 510 people in the short stay home and cared for 89 elders; the 2023–24 and 2024–25 reports both record 210 sheltered and 69 elders. The short stay home offers counselling, food and basic amenities, help with employment or family reunification, and skill and vocational training; the old age home provides regular health check-ups, medication support, access to doctors and nutritious meals. Separately, a de-addiction short stay home at Tharagampatti treated and counselled 57 young people in 2022–23.

Our care & rehabilitation pathway

  1. Shelter first Safe accommodation for people facing family crisis, homelessness or economic hardship, and for elders who are abandoned or neglected — with food, hygiene and security provided.
  2. Medical care on site Regular health check-ups, medication support and access to doctors, with help managing chronic conditions through partnerships with local healthcare providers.
  3. Counselling and emotional support Professional counsellors work with residents on trauma, family issues and emotional distress; social and emotional engagement reduces the isolation elderly residents live with.
  4. Nutrition Regular, balanced meals prepared with care, tailored to the dietary needs of elderly residents — at the PLHA home, a well-equipped kitchen provided nutritional food as part of treatment.
  5. Reintegration Skill development and vocational training to build self-reliance, plus assistance with employment or family reunification so that residents can leave.

Why this work matters

The challenge on the ground — and our answer

The challenge

Addiction with no route to recovery

Without a residential place to go, drug dependence goes untreated — and young people and their families are left to face it alone.

Our response

A dedicated de-addiction home

A de-addiction short stay home at Tharagampatti where 57 young people stayed in 2022–23 for treatment and counselling. In 2008–09, 25 patients at the Mylampatti centre were given comprehensive care for prevention, treatment and rehabilitation of addiction.

The challenge

No safe place for people living with HIV/AIDS

During HIV/AIDS training with SHG animators, members began naming people living with HIV/AIDS in their own field areas — family members and neighbours with nowhere to stay, recover or be treated.

Our response

A short stay home built for PLHA

Ten beds, a laboratory, a kitchen and separate wards for women and men, opened at Mylampatti in November 2007 and staffed by a part-time doctor, nurses, a counsellor, outreach workers and a lab technician.

The challenge

Medical treatment alone does not hold

Recovery falters when physical treatment is not matched with mental health support, and when the family around the patient is left out of it.

Our response

Counselling woven into every stage

Counselling runs alongside medical care in every home we operate — and at the Mylampatti centre in 2008–09, 57 family members were counselled on health and family issues alongside the patients themselves.

The challenge

Recovery ends at the community’s door

Even after treatment, people struggle to return to everyday life without an income, a family to return to or a community that will have them back.

Our response

Reintegration, not just recovery

Skill development and vocational training inside the home, plus assistance with employment or family reunification, so that a short stay is genuinely short.

Impact

What health support & rehabilitation changes

Shelter at scale

510 people sheltered in the short stay home and 89 elders cared for in the old age home in 2020–21 — the largest single year the reports record.

De-addiction support

A de-addiction home at Tharagampatti where 57 young people stayed for treatment and counselling in 2022–23, continuing work that began with 25 patients at the Mylampatti centre in 2008–09.

Care for people living with HIV/AIDS

A ten-bed home with its own laboratory and kitchen, opened in November 2007 for members and known contacts of the women’s Self-Help Groups the trust works with. The trust’s earlier website recorded 104 PLHA and 37 children infected or affected by HIV treated at its health centre; neither figure appears in an annual report.

Families, not just patients

57 family members were counselled on health and family issues at the Mylampatti centre in 2008–09 — treatment extended to the household around the person receiving it.
