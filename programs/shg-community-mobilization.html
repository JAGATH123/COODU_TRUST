SHG & Community Mobilization

Organizing women into Self-Help Groups for collective action and community development

Understanding SHG & Community Mobilization

Self-Help Groups (SHGs) and community mobilization represent a powerful approach to grassroots development that brings women together to address their common challenges through collective action. SHGs are small groups of women who come together to save money, access credit, and support each other in various development activities. Community mobilization involves organizing these groups and linking them with broader development initiatives.

Our SHG & Community Mobilization programs focus on forming, strengthening, and sustaining women's collectives that serve as platforms for economic empowerment, social transformation, and community development. Through the SHG model, women gain access to financial services, develop leadership skills, and become agents of change in their communities.

Key Components of SHG & Community Mobilization:

  • SHG Formation: Organizing women into small, cohesive groups of 10-15 members for mutual support and collective action
  • Capacity Building: Training SHG members in group management, financial literacy, and leadership skills
  • Savings & Credit: Facilitating group savings and providing access to credit for productive activities
  • Community Mobilization: Linking SHGs with community development programs and government schemes
  • Social Empowerment: Promoting women's participation in decision-making and community governance

Our SHG & Community Mobilization Projects (2009-2024)

Mahalir Thittam Project

2009-2015
Core Government Program

A core program promoting the economic and social empowerment of poor women through the SHG model. This comprehensive initiative under the Mahalir Thittam scheme focused on organizing women into sustainable Self-Help Groups, providing them with access to micro-credit, and building their capacity for collective action and community development.

SHG & Community Mobilization Impact

Group Formation

Successfully formed and sustained 450+ Self-Help Groups with over 6,750 women members, creating strong community networks for mutual support.

Savings Mobilization

Linked SHGs with banks and financial institutions, enabling members to access formal credit and banking services for the first time.

Developed over 1,000 women leaders who manage SHGs effectively and represent their communities in various development forums.

Social Change

Facilitated social transformation through collective action, addressing issues like education, health, and women's rights in communities.

Community Development

Mobilized communities for participatory development, implementing various infrastructure and social development projects through SHG networks.
