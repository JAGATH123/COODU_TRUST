Social empowerment & leadership

Building confident leaders and empowering women for social transformation — leadership, legal awareness and governance training for women's groups across Tamil Nadu.

4,200 PRI & SHG training participants, 2020–21
2,200 Groups & federations trained, 2018–19
60 Women SHG leaders trained, 2023–24
11 Districts with Coodu Trust SHGs

Understanding

What social empowerment & leadership means

What is social empowerment & leadership?

Social empowerment and leadership development for women involves building their confidence, skills, and capacity to participate meaningfully in social, economic, and political processes — and creating platforms for women to voice their concerns and contribute to community development.

Our programmes focus on transforming women from beneficiaries to leaders, enabling them to take charge of their lives and communities. The trust’s 2002–03 report describes women's groups being trained regularly on legal awareness, income generation programmes, biodiversity conservation and rural development, and emerging as independent bodies in the village. That work continues: 2,200 self-help groups, panchayat level federations, village panchayat resource centres and community resource persons were trained across five blocks of Virudhunagar in 2018–19, and 4,200 participants from government departments and SHGs in Virudhunagar, Ramanathapuram and Tirunelveli in 2020–21.

We enhance women's social role and status, particularly in the agricultural and environmental spheres where their contributions are often undervalued. The trust’s earlier website recorded 2,696 women benefiting from capacity building trainings; that total does not appear in any annual report, but the training itself is documented year after year — most recently 60 women SHG leaders trained in group management and enterprise development in Manur block in 2023–24.

Our empowerment pathway

  1. Mobilize women's groups Organizing rural women through self-help groups and strong community networks for mutual support and collective action.
  2. Build leadership & confidence Developing leadership skills, decision-making abilities and self-confidence among rural women.
  3. Raise legal awareness Educating women about their legal rights, property rights and available legal support systems.
  4. Link to income generation Connecting empowerment with economic opportunities for sustainable livelihood development.

Why this work matters

The challenge on the ground — and our answer

The challenge

Women's farm work goes undervalued

In the agricultural and environmental spheres, women's contributions are often undervalued and unrecognized.

Our response

Strengthen women's role in agriculture

Our Feminization of Agriculture campaign treated rural women as the best environmental managers in any region. Women's groups in Madurai, Theni and Dindigul were trained to promote homestead herbal gardens, and whole villages in Dindigul district took up vermicompost preparation run by women alone.

The challenge

Rights that stay on paper

Without legal literacy, rural women cannot understand or exercise their rights, access legal support, or advocate for gender equality.

Our response

Legal awareness women can use

Women's groups are trained regularly on legal awareness alongside income generation, biodiversity conservation and rural development. At the 2008 World Women's Day gathering at Tharagampatti the groups themselves passed 18 resolutions on women's rights.

The challenge

Left out of decision-making

Without confidence and platforms, women stay excluded from decision-making at the household and community levels.

Our response

Train confident community leaders

Leadership training builds decision-making abilities and confidence. The PRI–SHG programme took 2,200 groups, federations and resource persons through village development planning and budgeting in Virudhunagar in 2018–19, and 4,200 participants across three districts in 2020–21.

The challenge

Empowerment without income fades

Leadership gains are hard to sustain when women lack pathways to economic independence.

Our response

Link leadership to livelihoods

Every leadership programme carries an income-generation component. The 2023–24 SHG leaders' training covered group management and enterprise development together, so that better-run groups convert directly into earnings.

Impact

What social empowerment & leadership changes

Women leaders emerged

4,200 participants from government departments and self-help groups completed the PRI–SHG training in Virudhunagar, Ramanathapuram and Tirunelveli in 2020–21, after 2,200 groups and federations in Virudhunagar in 2018–19.

Legal awareness

Groups trained on legal awareness took their own positions: 18 resolutions on women's rights were passed by the self-help groups at the 2008 World Women's Day gathering at Tharagampatti, attended by more than 3,000 members from 315 groups.

Agricultural recognition

Under the Mahila Kisan Sashaktikaran Programme, 5,000 women farmers were identified and profiled across 37 villages of Thonthoni and Kadavur blocks, and 2,134 were trained in organic agriculture.

Economic empowerment

Linked leadership development with income generation opportunities — the trained groups went on to run community herbal nurseries, vermicomposting and mushroom production centres.
