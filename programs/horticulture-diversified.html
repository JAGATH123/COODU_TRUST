Understanding

What horticulture & diversified farming means

What is horticulture?

Horticulture is the branch of agriculture focusing on intensive cultivation of fruits, vegetables, flowers, spices, and specialty crops with higher returns per unit area.

Our approach focuses on introducing high-value crops, providing technical training in specialized cultivation techniques, and establishing market linkages for horticultural products through sustainable production practices that maximize yield while conserving natural resources. We have worked this way since 2003, when mushroom cultivation training first reached women’s groups in Dindigul, Trichy and Karur.

Our programs include modern cultivation techniques, post-harvest management, value addition, and developing entrepreneurial skills for horticultural enterprises that optimize land use and increase farm profitability. In 2013–14 alone we trained 1,267 mushroom entrepreneurs across five districts, 300 rural youth in nursery technology and 220 in coconut palm climbing.

Our horticulture development process

  1. Crop selection Identifying suitable high-value horticultural crops for local agro-climatic conditions.
  2. Technical training Providing specialized cultivation techniques, modern practices, and scientific methods.
  3. Production support Supporting farmers with quality inputs, equipment, and cultivation guidance.
  4. Market linkage Establishing market connections, post-harvest management, and value addition.

Why this work matters

The challenge on the ground — and our answer

The challenge

Low returns from traditional field crops

Families dependent on traditional low-value crops earn little from their land, with no clear path to a better income from the same acreage.

Our response

Introduce high-value horticultural crops

Fruits, vegetables, flowers and specialty crops earn more per unit area than traditional field crops, and mushrooms need very little land or capital at all.

The challenge

One crop, one harvest, one income

Single-crop systems concentrate a family's entire income into one harvest season — one failure can mean a lost year.

Our response

Diversify the cropping system

Crop rotation, mixed cropping and seasonal planning reduce risk and provide year-round income through different harvest seasons and shorter crop cycles.

The challenge

Rural women and youth without income skills

Without specialized skills, rural women and young people have few ways to earn a reliable daily income close to home.

Our response

Skill-based horticulture training

1,267 mushroom entrepreneurs trained in 2013–14 and 220 youth in coconut climbing — 72 of those youth now earn up to ₹750/day, and 41 women who took up mushroom cultivation after a 2023–24 training earn ₹300/day.

The challenge

Poor household nutrition

Farming families and school children often lack fresh fruits and vegetables in their daily diet.

Our response

Grow nutrition at home and school

Fresh fruits and vegetables improve household nutrition and food security — nutrient gardens established in 15 schools in Athoor and Nilakottai blocks, Dindigul district.

Impact

What horticulture & diversified farming changes

Specialty crop development

Introduced high-value specialty crops like mushrooms, providing farmers with profitable alternative cultivation options — 1,267 entrepreneurs trained in 2013–14 alone.

Skill-based training

Provided specialized technical training in coconut climbing and cultivation techniques for improved farm management.

Crop diversification

Enabled farmers to diversify their cropping systems and reduce dependence on traditional low-value crops.

Income enhancement

Increased farmer incomes through high-value horticultural crops with better market prices and shorter crop cycles.
