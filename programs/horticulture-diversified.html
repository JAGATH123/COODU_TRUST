Understanding

What horticulture & diversified farming means

What is horticulture?

Horticulture is the branch of agriculture focusing on intensive cultivation of fruits, vegetables, flowers, spices, and specialty crops with higher returns per unit area.

Our approach focuses on introducing high-value crops, providing technical training in specialized cultivation techniques, and establishing market linkages for horticultural products through sustainable production practices that maximize yield while conserving natural resources. Since 2003, 3,000+ beneficiaries have been trained across 5+ districts.

Our programs include modern cultivation techniques, post-harvest management, value addition, and developing entrepreneurial skills for horticultural enterprises that optimize land use and increase farm profitability — horticultural crops typically provide 3–5 times higher returns per unit area compared to traditional field crops.

  • Fruit cultivation
  • Vegetable production
  • Floriculture
  • Mushroom cultivation

Our horticulture development process

  1. Crop selection Identifying suitable high-value horticultural crops for local agro-climatic conditions.
  2. Technical training Providing specialized cultivation techniques, modern practices, and scientific methods.
  3. Production support Supporting farmers with quality inputs, equipment, and cultivation guidance.
  4. Market linkage Establishing market connections, post-harvest management, and value addition.

Why this work matters

The challenge on the ground — and our answer

The challenge

Low returns from traditional field crops

Families dependent on traditional low-value crops earn little from their land, with no clear path to a better income from the same acreage.

Our response

Introduce high-value horticultural crops

Fruits, vegetables, flowers and specialty crops typically provide 3-5 times higher returns per unit area compared to traditional field crops.

The challenge

One crop, one harvest, one income

Single-crop systems concentrate a family's entire income into one harvest season — one failure can mean a lost year.

Our response

Diversify the cropping system

Crop rotation, mixed cropping and seasonal planning reduce risk and provide year-round income through different harvest seasons and shorter crop cycles.

The challenge

Rural women and youth without income skills

Without specialized skills, rural women and young people have few ways to earn a reliable daily income close to home.

Our response

Skill-based horticulture training

1,612 women trained in mushroom cultivation and 220 youth in coconut climbing — 72 youth now earn up to ₹750/day and 41 women earn ₹300/day from their enterprises.

The challenge

Poor household nutrition

Farming families and school children often lack fresh fruits and vegetables in their daily diet.

Our response

Grow nutrition at home and school

Fresh fruits and vegetables improve household nutrition and food security — nutrient gardens established in 15 schools in Athoor and Nilakottai blocks, Dindigul district.
