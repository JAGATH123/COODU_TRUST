Agricultural technology & youth engagement

Introducing modern agricultural technologies and engaging rural youth in innovative farming practices.

750 Youth reached in 2015–16
75 Villages across five blocks
15 Training cum awareness programmes
60 Tractor operators trained, 2014–15

Understanding

What agricultural technology & youth engagement means

What is agricultural technology?

Agricultural technology is the application of modern tools, techniques, equipment and scientific methods to improve agricultural productivity, efficiency and sustainability — while youth engagement attracts young people to farming through innovative approaches and entrepreneurial opportunities.

Our programmes focus on bridging the gap between traditional farming and modern agricultural practice while making agriculture attractive to the younger generation. With technical support from the Tamil Nadu Agricultural University and Krishi Vigyan Kendra we began a precision farming demonstration with micro-irrigation on trust land, and in 2014–15 we trained 60 unemployed rural youths to operate tractors over 30 days, sponsored by the Star Club, Dindigul.

In 2015–16 the work scaled. Fifteen training cum awareness programmes on farm equipment, implements and drip and sprinkler irrigation reached about 750 youths from 75 villages across five blocks of Dindigul and Karur districts, alongside group formation to help young farmers reach distant markets, and a deliberate mix of enterprises: dairy for everyday income, goats and backyard poultry for money at any time, crops for periodic earnings, agroforestry trees for the long term, and honey, mushrooms and vermicompost for supplementary income.

  • Technology adoption
  • Digital literacy
  • Agri-entrepreneurship
  • Hands-on training

Key components of the programme

  1. Farm equipment and implements Popularising machinery and implements that make the work easier, and 30-day tractor operator training for rural youth.
  2. Precision farming with micro-irrigation A demonstration plot on trust land with technical support from TNAU and Krishi Vigyan Kendra, plus drip and sprinkler systems introduced to young farmers.
  3. A ladder of enterprises Dairy for everyday income, goats and backyard poultry for money at any time, crops for periodic earnings and agroforestry trees for the long term.
  4. Youth groups and market reach Forming groups among rural youth so they can reach distant markets, with honey, mushroom and vermicompost enterprises for supplementary income.

Why this work matters

The challenge on the ground — and our answer

The challenge

Young people leaving the farm

Agriculture struggles to attract the next generation — rural youth look outside farming for their future.

Our response

Make farming worth choosing

Attracting and Retaining Youth in Agriculture (2015–2016) set out to show that agriculture could be smart work with high returns — 15 training cum awareness programmes reaching about 750 youths in 75 villages of Dindigul and Karur.

The challenge

A gap between traditional and modern farming

Traditional methods alone cannot deliver the productivity, efficiency and sustainability that modern agriculture demands.

Our response

Train hands-on, on real technology

Training that popularised farm equipment, implements and drip and sprinkler irrigation, alongside a precision farming demonstration run with TNAU and Krishi Vigyan Kendra.

The challenge

One crop, one payday

A young farmer relying on a single seasonal crop waits months between payments — too long to build a living on.

Our response

Income on four timescales

We charted technologies for everyday income from dairy, money at any time from goats and backyard poultry, periodical earnings from crops and long-term income from agroforestry trees.

The challenge

Too small to reach a real market

A young farmer selling alone cannot reach the distant markets where produce is worth more.

Our response

Organise, then sell together

Group formation among rural youth to empower them for distant markets, with supplementary income from honey production, mushroom cultivation and vermicomposting.
