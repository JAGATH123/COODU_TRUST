Sustainable agriculture training

A Coodu Trust drought-response initiative that took sustainable agriculture workshops, seminars and publications to farming communities in officially declared drought-prone districts of Tamil Nadu.

  • Trust initiative
  • 2002–2003
  • Completed
2002–2003 Program duration (annual program)
4 Training modules covered
2 Institutional technical partners

The project

Returning to nature during a drought crisis

Project mission

Reintroducing sustainable agricultural concepts to combat water scarcity and farmland degradation by promoting natural soil fertility management and reducing chemical dependency during Tamil Nadu's severe drought period.

Implemented by Coodu Trust during 2002–2003 — a period marked by significant drought in Tamil Nadu — this drought response program targeted farming communities in districts officially declared as drought-prone by the government. Farmland in these areas had become fully chemicalised from overuse of synthetic inputs, and the program set out to return farming to nature.

Training was delivered through workshops, seminars and sustainable agriculture publications, with schedules adapted to local social preferences. Expert guidance came from the Gandhigram Rural Institute (GRI) and technical support from Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) — 2 institutional partners backing 4 training modules.

  • Eco-friendly farming
  • Natural soil fertility
  • Organic vs conventional
  • Knowledge dissemination

What the training covered

  1. Sustainable agriculture concepts Eco-friendly farming principles, environmental-economic balance and long-term productivity, via interactive workshops and seminars.
  2. Natural soil fertility management Bio-fertilizer application, soil rejuvenation and natural nutrient enhancement through hands-on training and demonstrations.
  3. Organic vs conventional farming Discussion forums and comparative studies on the benefits of organic methods for soil health and environmental quality.
  4. Knowledge dissemination Publications on sustainable agriculture released as lasting reference materials for continued learning.
  5. Workshop delivery A series of workshops and seminars conducted across drought-prone districts, with schedules adapted to local social preferences.

Why this work matters

The challenge on the ground — and our answer

The challenge

Chemicalised farmland in a drought year

Farmland described as fully chemicalised from overuse of synthetic inputs was degrading just as severe drought gripped Tamil Nadu — leaving soil with little natural fertility to fall back on.

Our response

Natural soil fertility, hands-on

Hands-on training and demonstrations in bio-fertilizer application, soil rejuvenation and natural nutrient enhancement — plus forums comparing organic and conventional systems — to rejuvenate degraded soil and cut chemical dependency.

The challenge

Training that ends when the seminar does

A one-off workshop leaves farming communities with nothing to refer back to once the trainers move on — and sessions that ignore local rhythms struggle to draw farmers in at all.

Our response

Publications and locally adapted schedules

Coodu Trust developed and released sustainable agriculture publications as lasting reference materials, adapted training schedules to local social preferences, and drew on GRI expert guidance and KVK technical support.
