Program Overview

The Western Ghats Development Programme (WGDP) was implemented by Coodu Trust in Virudhunagar District during 2001-2002 as part of the Central Government's initiative to protect and sustainably develop the Western Ghats biodiversity hotspot. The Western Ghats represent a UNESCO World Heritage Site with exceptional levels of endemism and critical ecosystems requiring specialized conservation approaches.

🎯 Programme Objectives

The WGDP aimed to balance biodiversity conservation with sustainable development by integrating watershed management, GIS technology, capacity building, and institutional partnerships to create a comprehensive eco-development framework for the Western Ghats region.

During the implementation period, Coodu Trust undertook comprehensive village-level GIS mapping covering 421 villages in Virudhunagar district, enabling evidence-based spatial planning and conservation management. This technological intervention was complemented by capacity building initiatives, socio-economic surveys, and strategic partnerships with the Tamil Nadu Forest Department in nearby Sivagangai district.

The programme was implemented in coordination with the Integrated Watershed Development Programme (IWDP), creating synergies between biodiversity conservation and community-based natural resource management. Documentation efforts included developing DRDA scheme brochures and publications to disseminate knowledge and promote sustainable practices.

Connected Conservation Initiatives

🌿 Ethno-Botanical Garden Initiative

As part of broader Western Ghats biodiversity conservation efforts, Coodu Trust established a 3-acre ethno-botanical garden at Mayilampatty field office in Karur district, complementing the WGDP's focus on protecting endemic flora.

🎯 Garden Objectives

Conserves medicinal plant species known to various ethnic communities, providing education and training to farmers, schools, colleges, folk healers, and women SHG groups.

📚 Knowledge Context

India has 2,500 plant species used by traditional healers, with 100 species serving as regular medicine sources. The garden preserves this invaluable traditional knowledge.
