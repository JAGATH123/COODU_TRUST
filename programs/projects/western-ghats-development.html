The project

Protecting a biodiversity hotspot through data and partnership

Project mission

Implementing the Western Ghats Development Programme (WGDP) in Virudhunagar district to protect one of the world's most important biodiversity hotspots while promoting sustainable development through GIS mapping, watershed integration and Forest Department collaboration.

The Western Ghats Development Programme is part of the Central Government's initiative to protect and sustainably develop the Western Ghats — a UNESCO World Heritage Site with exceptional levels of endemism. Coodu Trust delivered the programme in coordination with the District Rural Development Agency (DRDA), anchored by comprehensive village-level GIS mapping covering 421 villages in Virudhunagar district.

The programme was implemented in coordination with the Integrated Watershed Development Programme (IWDP), combining biodiversity conservation with community-based natural resource management. In collaboration with the Tamil Nadu Forest Department in Sivagangai district, and through a 3-acre ethno-botanical garden at the Mayilampatty field office in Karur, the work joined spatial planning, forest conservation and traditional knowledge in a single effort.

What the project delivered

  1. GIS mapping Digitization of 421 villages in Virudhunagar for spatial planning and biodiversity monitoring.
  2. Socio-economic survey (SES) Baseline data collection for planning and monitoring.
  3. Forest Department collaboration Partnership in Sivagangai district for integrated biodiversity conservation.
  4. IWDP integration Coordinated implementation with the Integrated Watershed Development Programme, combining conservation with community-based resource management.
  5. Ethno-botanical garden 3-acre garden at Mayilampatty (Karur) conserving medicinal plant species, with training for farmers, schools, colleges, folk healers and women SHG groups.
  6. Documentation & outreach DRDA scheme brochures, programme publications and socio-economic survey reports.

Why this work matters

The challenge on the ground — and our answer

The challenge

Conservation planning without spatial data

A biodiversity hotspot cannot be protected village by village without reliable spatial information — planners are left guessing where resources and pressures actually sit on the ground.

Our response

GIS mapping of 421 villages

Comprehensive village-level GIS mapping across 421 villages in Virudhunagar, giving evidence-based planning and a biodiversity monitoring framework for technology-driven conservation.

The challenge

Endemic species and traditional knowledge at risk

Endemic species and the medicinal plant knowledge held by ethnic communities can vanish when conservation works in isolation from forest agencies and local healers.

Our response

Forest partnership and an ethno-botanical garden

A strategic partnership with the Tamil Nadu Forest Department in Sivagangai, plus a 3-acre ethno-botanical garden at Mayilampatty conserving medicinal species and training farmers, schools, colleges, folk healers and women SHG groups.
