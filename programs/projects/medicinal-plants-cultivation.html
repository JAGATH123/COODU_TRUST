The project

From a conservation garden to women's livelihoods

Project mission

Preserving traditional medicinal plant knowledge and biodiversity through the establishment of an ethno-botanical garden, training women in medicinal plant cultivation and marketing, and creating livelihood opportunities through medicinal plant-based enterprises — bridging indigenous healing traditions with modern livelihood development.

India's traditional medicine systems — Ayurveda, Siddha and Unani, alongside folk medicine — draw upon approximately 2,500 plant species used by traditional healers, with over 100 species serving as regular sources of medicine. To conserve this heritage, Coodu Trust established a 3-acre ethno-botanical garden at its Mayilampatty field office in Karur district in 2002–2003 under the Biodiversity Conservation programme — a living repository serving farmers, schools, colleges, folk healers and women's SHG groups.

In October 2020 the Trust carried that conservation work into livelihoods: 200 women were trained at Velliyani, Karur district, over two 2-day sessions (7–8 October and 28–29 October 2020) across a 4-module curriculum spanning plant value, home gardens, nursery raising, and cultivation and marketing — an 18-year arc from conservation infrastructure to community livelihood application. The Trust is also a member of the LEISA Network for sustainable agriculture, including medicinal plants.

What the training covered

  1. Introduction to medicinal plants & their value India's traditional medicine heritage; Ayurveda, Siddha and Unani; identifying local species and their therapeutic uses.
  2. Medicinal plants & the home garden Design, species selection, companion planting and seasonal planning for household health needs.
  3. Nursery raising for medicinal plants Seed collection, propagation methods, nursery beds, seedling care and commercial nursery management.
  4. Cultivation & marketing Field cultivation, organic pest management, optimal harvesting, post-harvest handling and market channels.

Why this work matters

The challenge on the ground — and our answer

The challenge

Traditional plant knowledge without a home

The medicinal plant knowledge of the region's ethnic communities — the basis of Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani and folk medicine — has no permanent living repository where it can be conserved and taught.

Our response

A 3-acre ethno-botanical garden

Established at Mayilampatty in 2002–2003 under the Biodiversity Conservation programme — a living repository of species known to ethnic communities, serving farmers, schools, colleges, folk healers and women's SHG groups.

The challenge

Plants with value, but no pathway to income

Rural women live among marketable medicinal species yet lack the cultivation, nursery and marketing skills to turn them into household health security or enterprise.

Our response

A four-module livelihood curriculum

200 women trained at Velliyani over two 2-day sessions in October 2020 — from home gardens for family health to nursery raising, commercial cultivation and market channels.

Outcomes

What the project changed

A living conservation garden

The 3-acre ethno-botanical garden at Mayilampatty conserves medicinal species known to the region's ethnic communities — a permanent educational resource for multiple community groups.

Home gardens & enterprise skills

200 trained women can grow medicinal species in their backyards for family health — and carry commercial nursery, cultivation and marketing skills from home garden to micro-enterprise.

Conservation-to-production ecosystem

The conservation garden at Mayilampatty and the production nursery at Velliyani form a complete ecosystem — from species conservation and research to training and commercial production, all within Karur district.
