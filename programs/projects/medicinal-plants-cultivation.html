Preserving traditional medicinal plant knowledge and biodiversity through the establishment of an ethno-botanical garden, training women in medicinal plant cultivation and marketing, and creating livelihood opportunities through medicinal plant-based enterprises — bridging indigenous healing traditions with modern livelihood development.

Programme Overview

Coodu Trust's medicinal plants programme has two interconnected components: a permanent conservation infrastructure (the 3-acre ethno-botanical garden at Mayilampatty) and ongoing training programmes for women and community groups on medicinal plant cultivation, home gardening, and marketing.

🌿 Core Context

Plants have been used in traditional medicine for several thousand years. India's traditional medicine systems — Ayurveda, Unani, and Siddha — draw upon approximately 2,500 plant species used by traditional healers, with over 100 species serving as regular sources of medicine. Tamil Nadu's Siddha system in particular has deep roots in local plant biodiversity. Preserving this knowledge at the community level is both a conservation imperative and a livelihood opportunity.

The centrepiece conservation initiative is the 3-acre ethno-botanical garden established at the Mayilampatty field office in Karur district. This garden conserves medicinal plant species known to various ethnic communities of the region, serving as both a living repository and an educational resource centre. The garden is managed by Coodu Trust and provides education and training to diverse user groups including farmers, schools, colleges, folk healers, and women's Self-Help Groups.

Building on this conservation foundation, Coodu Trust conducted a dedicated medicinal plants cultivation training programme at Velliyani in October 2020, training 200 women over two sessions (7-8 October and 28-29 October 2020). The training was held at Velliyani where a medicinal plant nursery had been developed, providing hands-on learning alongside classroom instruction.

Key Activities & Achievements

🌿

Ethno-Botanical Garden (3 Acres, Mayilampatty)

3 Acres | Karur District | Ongoing Resource

A dedicated 3-acre ethno-botanical garden was established at the Mayilampatty field office in Karur district. The garden conserves medicinal plant species known to various ethnic communities of the region, serving as a permanent living repository of traditional botanical knowledge. The garden is managed by Coodu Trust and functions as an active educational resource for multiple community groups.

🎓

Women's Cultivation Training (Velliyani, 2020)

200 Women | 2 Sessions | Velliyani Nursery

A comprehensive training programme was conducted at Velliyani, where a medicinal plant nursery had been developed, over two sessions in October 2020. The curriculum covered four key modules: Introduction to Medicinal Plants and their Value, Medicinal Plants and Home Garden establishment, Nursery Raising of Medicinal Plants, and Cultivation and Marketing of Medicinal Plants.

🏠

Home Garden Development

Training Module: Medicinal Plants & Home Garden

Women were trained specifically on establishing medicinal plant home gardens — enabling each household to grow commonly used medicinal species in their own backyard for family health needs. This approach democratises access to traditional remedies, reduces healthcare costs, and creates small-scale livelihood opportunities through surplus sale.

📈

Nursery & Marketing Skills

Training Modules:

  • • Nursery Raising — Medicinal plants propagation and management
  • • Cultivation techniques — soil, water, spacing, harvesting
  • • Marketing of Medicinal Plants — market identification, pricing, supply chains

Beyond home use, the training equipped women with commercial skills — raising medicinal plant nurseries for sale, cultivating marketable species, and understanding market channels for medicinal plant products. This creates a pathway from home garden to micro-enterprise.

Training Curriculum (Velliyani, October 2020)

A comprehensive four-module curriculum covering traditional knowledge, home gardens, nursery techniques, and market linkages.

MODULE 1

Introduction to Medicinal Plants and Their Value

  • Overview of India's traditional medicine heritage
  • Ayurveda, Siddha, and Unani systems and their plant basis
  • Identification of locally available medicinal species
  • Health benefits and therapeutic applications of common plants
  • Role of ethnic community knowledge in preserving plant medicine traditions
MODULE 2

Medicinal Plants and Home Garden

  • Design and layout of a medicinal plants home garden
  • Selection of species for household health needs
  • Companion planting and garden management
  • Seasonal planning for year-round medicinal plant availability
  • Integration with kitchen garden and nutrition gardens
MODULE 3

Nursery Raising — Medicinal Plants

  • Seed collection and storage for medicinal species
  • Propagation methods — seeds, cuttings, division, tissue culture basics
  • Nursery bed preparation and growing media
  • Seedling care, hardening, and transplanting
  • Nursery management for commercial production
MODULE 4

Cultivation and Marketing of Medicinal Plants

  • Field cultivation techniques for commercial medicinal species
  • Soil preparation, irrigation, and organic pest management
  • Harvesting at optimal stage for maximum medicinal value
  • Post-harvest handling and processing
  • Market identification, pricing, and supply chain development
