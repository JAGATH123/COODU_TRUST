Jatropha Cultivation Programme

Promoting Bio-Diesel Crop Cultivation for Sustainable Livelihoods

Programme Overview

The Jatropha Cultivation Programme was a strategic initiative by Coodu Trust to promote the cultivation of Jatropha curcas — known locally as "Kattamanakku" in Tamil — as a sustainable biofuel crop and livelihood option for farming communities. This initiative was aligned with the Government of India's National Biofuel Policy and the broader national mission to develop biodiesel as an alternative energy source.

🎯 Core Strategy

Leverage the drought-tolerant and wasteland-friendly characteristics of Jatropha curcas to provide farmers with a viable supplementary income source while simultaneously contributing to India's energy security and the productive reclamation of marginal and degraded lands.

Coodu Trust took a proactive initiative to promote Jatropha cultivation among farmers across various districts of Tamil Nadu. The programme involved large-scale distribution of quality Jatropha seedlings to farming communities, complemented by technical guidance on cultivation practices, land preparation, and crop management on wasteland and marginal lands.

Jatropha curcas is a multi-purpose shrub of significant economic importance, valued for its oil-rich seeds which yield 27-40% oil content suitable for biodiesel production. The plant is well-adapted to semi-arid and marginal lands, requires minimal water, and can serve as a live fence while not being browsed by animals — making it an ideal crop for wasteland reclamation and sustainable land use in drought-prone regions.

The programme was implemented in close coordination with government departments including the Agriculture Department and Horticulture Department, as part of Coodu Trust's broader environmental conservation and sustainable livelihood strategy.

Key Activities & Achievements

🌱

Large-Scale Seedling Distribution

2,00,000 Seedlings
Multiple Districts

Nearly 200,000 Jatropha seedlings were distributed to farmers across various districts of Tamil Nadu. This large-scale seedling distribution programme provided farming communities with quality planting material to establish Jatropha cultivation on their marginal and wasteland areas, enabling them to participate in the emerging biofuel economy.

🏜️

Wasteland Cultivation Promotion

500 Hectares
Promoted

Coodu Trust took the initiative to promote Jatropha cultivation across 500 hectares of land. The focus was on utilising wasteland and marginal lands that were not suitable for conventional food crop cultivation, thereby converting unproductive land into economically viable biofuel plantations without competing with food production.

🎓

Technical Guidance & Training

Focus Areas:

  • • Land selection and site preparation for Jatropha cultivation
  • • Planting techniques and spacing for optimal yield
  • • Crop management and maintenance practices
  • • Harvesting methods and seed processing

Farmers received comprehensive technical guidance on Jatropha cultivation practices, covering the entire crop cycle from land preparation through planting, maintenance, and eventual harvesting. Training emphasised the low-input, drought-tolerant nature of the crop and its suitability for marginal lands.

🌍

Environmental & Economic Benefits

Dual Impact:

  • • Wasteland reclamation and soil erosion prevention
  • • Carbon sequestration through tree cover on degraded lands
  • • Potential biodiesel income for farming households
  • • Live fencing and boundary planting benefits

The programme created dual environmental and economic benefits — converting degraded wastelands into productive land while providing farmers with a potential supplementary income source through biodiesel feedstock production, contributing to both land restoration and rural livelihoods.

National Policy Context

India's National Biofuel Policy envisioned that approximately 20% of the country's domestic diesel demand would be met by biofuels, with Jatropha cultivation identified as a crucial component. The Government of India identified 400,000 square kilometres of wasteland suitable for Jatropha cultivation. Tamil Nadu was among the states aggressively promoting Jatropha plantation to help farmers overcome losses due to irregular rainfall.

Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam, the 11th President of India — with whom Coodu Trust's Director has been photographed — was one of the strongest advocates of Jatropha cultivation for biodiesel production, citing the suitability of India's vast wasteland resources for this purpose.

Coodu Trust's Jatropha programme was aligned with this national vision, contributing at the grassroots level by enabling farming communities to participate in India's biofuel mission while addressing local challenges of wasteland reclamation and sustainable livelihoods.
