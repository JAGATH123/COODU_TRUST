Program Overview

The Rainwater Harvesting (RWH) Program was a timely and critical initiative launched to address the severe drought conditions experienced in Tamil Nadu between 2001 and 2003. This foundational program marked Coodu Trust's early commitment to water conservation and community resilience.

🎯 Primary Objectives

Promote widespread adoption of rainwater harvesting techniques to conserve water, recharge critically depleted groundwater sources, and mitigate the impacts of drought on rural communities through comprehensive awareness and demonstration programs.

By empowering local communities with knowledge and demonstrating effective RWH methods, the program aimed to enhance water security and build long-term environmental sustainability. This initiative was strategically aligned with a larger, state-mandated movement.

The program's success contributed to the landmark government ordinance in 2003 that made RWH compulsory for all buildings in Tamil Nadu, demonstrating the critical role of grassroots initiatives in influencing state-level policy and creating sustainable water conservation practices.

Key Activities & Achievements (2002–2003)

📢

Awareness Campaigns

Coodu Trust conducted extensive awareness programs across operational villages to educate communities on the importance and techniques of rainwater harvesting.

🎯 Campaign Focus Areas

  • 💧 Water Conservation: Sustainable agriculture and domestic use
  • 🌱 Long-term Benefits: Environmental sustainability and resilience
  • 📚 Knowledge Transfer: Practical RWH techniques and methods
🔧

Practical Demonstrations

The program included practical demonstrations of various RWH techniques, showcasing effective methods to capture and store rainwater.

🛠️ Demonstration Techniques

  • ☔ Water Capture: Effective rainwater collection methods
  • 🏺 Storage Systems: Various storage and conservation techniques
  • 🔄 Groundwater Recharge: Reducing dependency on over-exploited sources
👥

Community & School Participation

The initiative actively involved key community stakeholders, fostering collective responsibility for water conservation.

🤝 Key Stakeholders

👨‍🌾 Farmers: Primary agricultural community engagement
👩 Rural Women: Household water management leaders
🏛️ Local Panchayats: Governance and policy implementation

📊 Program Legacy & Impact

This foundation program focused on qualitative impact through awareness and demonstration rather than quantitative metrics. The success is evidenced by:

  • State-wide policy influence: Contributed to the 2003 government mandate making RWH compulsory
  • Community behavioral shift: Established foundation for widespread water conservation practices
  • Groundwater improvement: Part of the broader movement that led to measurable water table recovery
  • Institutional learning: Provided critical insights for Coodu Trust's subsequent water management projects
