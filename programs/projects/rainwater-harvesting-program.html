Rainwater harvesting program

A Coodu Trust drought-response initiative delivered across operational villages in multiple districts of Tamil Nadu — the foundation water conservation programme that helped pave the way for the 2003 state-wide RWH mandate.

The project

What this project set out to do

Project mission

A timely and critical initiative to address severe drought conditions in Tamil Nadu through widespread adoption of rainwater harvesting techniques — conserving water, recharging groundwater sources, and building community resilience.

Launched to address the severe drought conditions experienced in Tamil Nadu between 2001 and 2003, this drought response initiative was implemented by Coodu Trust across operational villages in multiple districts, in the domain of the Municipal Administration & Water Supply department. The 2002–2003 programme deliberately focused on demonstration and awareness rather than construction counts — teaching communities how to capture, store and use rain where it falls.

The programme actively involved key community stakeholders — farmers, rural women and local panchayats — fostering collective responsibility for water conservation. Strategically aligned with a larger, state-mandated movement, its success contributed to the landmark government ordinance in 2003 that made RWH compulsory for all buildings in Tamil Nadu.

What the programme delivered

  1. Awareness campaigns Extensive awareness programmes across operational villages on the importance and techniques of rainwater harvesting.
  2. Practical demonstrations Demonstrations of various RWH techniques, showcasing effective methods to capture and store rainwater.
  3. Knowledge transfer Practical RWH techniques and methods passed on to households and farmers.
  4. Storage & recharge systems Various storage and conservation techniques, reducing dependency on over-exploited groundwater sources.
  5. Community & school participation Farmers, rural women and local panchayats engaged to foster collective responsibility for water conservation.

Why this work matters

The challenge on the ground — and our answer

The challenge

Severe drought and over-exploited groundwater

Between 2001 and 2003 Tamil Nadu faced severe drought, with communities dependent on over-exploited groundwater sources and little awareness of how to capture the rain that did fall.

Our response

Awareness and demonstration at village level

Extensive awareness campaigns and practical demonstrations of RWH techniques across operational villages — effective methods to capture and store rainwater and recharge groundwater.

The challenge

Water conservation without community ownership

Conservation efforts rarely last when villagers see them as someone else's responsibility — lasting change needs farmers, women and panchayats to take collective charge.

Our response

Stakeholder engagement that shaped policy

Farmers, rural women and local panchayats were actively involved, fostering collective responsibility — and the programme's success contributed to the 2003 ordinance making RWH compulsory for all buildings in Tamil Nadu.

Outcomes

What the project changed

Increased awareness

Educated farmers and households on the long-term benefits of rainwater harvesting — a community behavioural shift that laid the foundation for widespread water conservation practices.

Groundwater recharge

Contributed to aquifer recharge and well revival, as part of the broader movement that led to measurable water table recovery.

Policy influence

Contributed to the 2003 state-wide mandate making RWH compulsory — and provided critical insights for Coodu Trust's subsequent water management projects.
