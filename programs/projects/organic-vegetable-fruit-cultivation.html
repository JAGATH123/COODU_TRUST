🌱 ORGANIC FARMING TECHNOLOGY PROGRAMME

Organic Vegetable & Fruit Cultivation Training

Residue-Free Vegetable & Mango Production through KVK Dindigul (2014-2015)

🎯 Program Mission

Disseminating organic input-based agriculture technologies for the production of nutrient-rich, residue-free vegetables and fruits, replacing high chemical-demanding existing technologies with alternate organic methods — enabling farmers to produce safer food, protect soil health, reduce input costs, and access premium markets for organic produce.

Programme Overview

The Organic Vegetable and Fruit Cultivation Training programme was implemented by Coodu Trust during 2014-2015 in partnership with Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK), Dindigul. The programme was designed to disseminate organic input-based agriculture technologies specifically for the production of nutrient-rich and residue-free vegetables and fruits, addressing the growing concern about chemical contamination in food and the degradation of soil health from excessive synthetic input use.

🎯 Core Strategy

Replace existing high chemical-demanding agricultural technologies with alternate organic input-based methods that are equally productive but environmentally sustainable — demonstrating to farmers that chemical-free farming is not only possible but commercially viable, producing residue-free food that commands premium market prices while restoring soil health and protecting the environment.

The programme targeted two distinct crop-specific zones within Dindigul district: Oddanchatram block for intensive vegetable production and Natham block for mango cultivation. This strategic selection ensured that organic technologies were demonstrated in areas where they would have the most immediate relevance and impact — Oddanchatram being one of Tamil Nadu's largest vegetable marketing hubs, and Natham being a significant mango-growing area.

A total of 750 farmers were trained in organic technologies covering bio-fertilisers, bio-control agents, use of traps, and cultural and mechanical means of pest and disease control. The alternate organic input-based technologies were specifically designed to replace the high chemical-demanding existing technologies that these farmers had been using, providing practical, cost-effective substitutes.

Key Activities & Achievements

🥬

Vegetable Production — Oddanchatram Block

Focus: Residue-Free Vegetable Cultivation

Farmers in Oddanchatram block — one of Tamil Nadu's most important vegetable marketing centres — were trained in organic technologies for producing nutrient-rich, residue-free vegetables. The training provided practical alternatives to chemical-intensive vegetable farming, demonstrating that organic methods can produce commercially competitive yields while eliminating pesticide residues from food.

🥭

Mango Cultivation — Natham Block

Focus: Organic Mango Production Technologies

Mango growers in Natham block received specialised training in organic input-based mango cultivation technologies. The programme addressed the specific pest and disease management challenges of mango farming, providing organic alternatives to the heavy chemical spray schedules traditionally used in commercial mango production.

🔬

Organic Input Technologies

Technologies Covered:

  • • Bio-fertiliser application — natural nutrient sources replacing synthetic fertilisers
  • • Bio-control agents — biological pest management replacing chemical pesticides
  • • Trap technologies — pheromone and light traps for pest monitoring and control
  • • Cultural methods — crop rotation, intercropping, companion planting
  • • Mechanical pest control — physical removal and barrier methods

The training provided comprehensive exposure to the full spectrum of organic farming technologies, covering biological, cultural, and mechanical methods for both soil fertility management and pest/disease control. Each technology was presented as a direct replacement for specific chemical inputs that farmers were currently using.

🎓

KVK Technical Partnership

Partner: Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK), Dindigul

Collaboration Benefits:

  • • Research-backed training content from agricultural scientists
  • • Demonstration of proven organic technologies
  • • Location-specific recommendations for Dindigul conditions
  • • Ongoing technical support for adopting farmers
  • • Connection to KVK's extension network

Krishi Vigyan Kendra, Dindigul — the district's agricultural science centre — served as the technical knowledge partner, ensuring that all organic technologies taught were scientifically validated, locally tested, and practically applicable for the specific agro-climatic conditions of Oddanchatram and Natham blocks.

Why Oddanchatram & Natham Were Strategic Choices

🥬 Oddanchatram — Tamil Nadu's Vegetable Hub

Oddanchatram in Dindigul district is one of the largest vegetable wholesale markets in Tamil Nadu, with thousands of farmers producing vegetables for this market. The heavy use of chemical pesticides and fertilisers in intensive vegetable farming had made this area a prime candidate for organic conversion — the market infrastructure was already in place, and organic produce commands premium prices.

🥭 Natham — Mango Country

Natham block is a significant mango-growing area in Dindigul district. Commercial mango farming relies heavily on chemical sprays for pest and disease management. Converting these farmers to organic methods directly improves the quality and safety of mango production while reducing environmental contamination in a horticulturally important zone.

Context — Coodu Trust's Organic Farming Portfolio

This 2014-15 programme was distinctive because it was crop-specific (vegetables and mango), location-specific (Oddanchatram and Natham), and technology-replacement focused (directly substituting chemical inputs with organic alternatives), making it more targeted than the broader organic farming trainings.
