NWDPRA Overview

The National Watershed Development Programme for Rainfed Areas (NWDPRA) was a national scheme implemented by Coodu Trust between 2002 and 2004. The program was specifically designed to enhance agricultural productivity and sustainability in rainfed regions through a comprehensive watershed management approach. The core objective was the holistic development of villages on a watershed basis, aiming to restore ecological balance by conserving and developing degraded natural resources, particularly land and water, with a strong emphasis on community participation.

🎯 Core Strategy

Implementation of comprehensive watershed management to enhance agricultural productivity and sustainability in rainfed regions, focusing on holistic village development and ecological balance restoration.

The implementation framework was centered around empowering local communities. Coodu Trust served as the key implementing agency, facilitating the formation of grassroots institutions such as Watershed Associations, Watershed Committees, User Groups, and Self-Help Groups (SHGs). During this period, the program's activities were concentrated in the rainfed areas of Dindigul (Thoppampatty block) and Madurai (North, South, and other blocks), targeting farmers and local communities who were most vulnerable to the challenges of rainfed agriculture.

A central tenet of the NWDPRA was empowering local communities to take ownership of their resources. This was achieved by establishing grassroots institutions like Watershed Associations, Watershed Committees, and User Groups, ensuring that all development activities were planned and executed with direct community involvement.
