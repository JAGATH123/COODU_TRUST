Training on coconut climbing

A skill-based livelihood programme designed by Coodu Trust for rural youth in Tamil Nadu — professional palm climbing with modern devices, with TNAU and the Coconut Development Board as technology partners.

  • Trust initiative
  • 2013–2014
220 Rural youth trained
7 Training programmes
72 Now professional climbers
33% Employment conversion rate

The project

What this project set out to do

Project mission

Address the critical shortage of skilled labour for coconut palm cleaning and harvesting in Tamil Nadu by training rural youth in professional palm climbing using modern climbing devices — creating a high-income livelihood that tackles one of the biggest constraints in the coconut farming sector.

Designed and implemented by Coodu Trust in Tamil Nadu during 2013–2014, the programme trained 220 rural youth through 7 training programmes, each tailored for 5 days of intensive, hands-on practice. Participants were unemployed and underemployed rural youth, members of coconut farming communities and aspiring self-employed youth.

Trainees learned two complementary modern climbing devices — one designed by the Tamil Nadu Agricultural University (TNAU) and another popularised by the Coconut Development Board (CDB), a statutory body under the Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare, Government of India. The devices enable safe, efficient climbing without the strain and risk of traditional barefoot climbing, letting a skilled climber service ~40–50 trees a day.

The context was urgent: the area under coconut cultivation was diminishing and productivity declining in Tamil Nadu due to drought, fluctuating rainfall and a severe skilled-labour shortage for palm cleaning and nut harvesting. Trained climbers become self-employed with zero capital investment beyond the climbing device itself.

What the training covered

  1. Structured 5-day programme Classroom instruction on safety, tree biology and equipment handling, plus practical sessions on actual palms.
  2. Modern climbing devices Hands-on training on the TNAU-designed device and the CDB device.
  3. Palm crown cleaning Crown cleaning and removal of dead fronds that harbour pests.
  4. Nut harvesting Harvesting at optimal maturity for maximum value.
  5. Tree health assessment Tree health assessment and pest identification.
  6. Servicing efficiency Efficiency training so a skilled climber can service ~40–50 trees daily.

Why this work matters

The challenge on the ground — and our answer

The challenge

No skilled hands to climb the palms

Coconut cultivation in Tamil Nadu was shrinking and productivity declining — drought, fluctuating rainfall and a severe shortage of skilled labour for palm cleaning and nut harvesting left farmers unable to maintain their trees.

Our response

220 youth trained as professional climbers

Seven 5-day training programmes combined classroom instruction on safety, tree biology and equipment handling with extensive practice on actual coconut palms — covering crown cleaning, nut harvesting and tree health assessment.

The challenge

Barefoot climbing: strain, risk, few takers

Traditional barefoot climbing carries physical strain and risk — leaving unemployed and underemployed rural youth without a safe, viable path into this in-demand trade.

Our response

Modern devices that turn climbing into a career

Training on the TNAU and CDB climbing devices made climbing safe and efficient — 72 trained youth adopted it as their primary profession, earning up to ₹750 per day at ₹15–20 per tree, with the device (~₹2,000–5,000) as the only capital required.

Outcomes

What the project changed

72 youth made climbing their profession

72 of the 220 trained youth — a 33% conversion rate — adopted coconut climbing as their primary livelihood, self-employed with no capital beyond the climbing device (~₹2,000–5,000).

Up to ₹750 a day in earnings

Charging ₹15–20 per tree, trained climbers earn up to ₹750 per day — roughly ₹18,750 a month over 25 working days, with annual income potential of ~₹2,25,000.

Year-round demand, sector-wide gains

Coconuts are harvested every 45–60 days, so each climber can service 100+ farming households year-round — regular harvesting lifts tree productivity and farmer income, and keeps youth working in their own communities.
