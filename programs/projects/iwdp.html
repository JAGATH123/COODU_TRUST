Integrated watershed development programme (IWDP)

A government-sponsored watershed scheme in which Coodu Trust served as coordinating agency across seven districts of Tamil Nadu — conserving soil, water and biomass to restore ecological balance and sustain rural livelihoods.

The project

Treating whole watersheds — land, water and people together

Project mission

A government-sponsored scheme for holistic watershed development, focusing on conserving essential natural resources like soil, water and biomass to restore ecological balance and create sustainable livelihood opportunities.

Under the Integrated Watershed Development Programme (IWDP), Coodu Trust worked from 2001 as the coordinating agency alongside the Project Implementing Agencies and District Rural Development Agencies. The 2001–02 and 2002–03 activity tables record IWDP work in 127 watersheds across seven districts — Dindigul (Shanarpatty and Vedasandur), Karur (Kadavur and Thogamalai), Madurai (Alanganallur), Theni (Periakulam), Trichy (Thathaiyangarpettai), Tirunelveli (Nanguneri and Kalakkadu) and Sivagangai. By 2003–04 the scheme covered 126 watersheds, with the report naming Dindigul, Karur, Madurai, Tirunelveli and Sivagangai.

Everything ran through a three-tiered governance structure. In 2001–02 a watershed association and a watershed committee were formed in 115 of those watersheds, and 10,270 people were organised into user groups and 7,340 into self-help groups — a network of community institutions serving the rural resource-poor, farmers and other community members. The reports count participants, not groups: these are people brought into village institutions.

The work continued well past the first phase. In 2007–08, under IWDP – IV in Vadamadurai block, Dindigul, 160 members from 20 self-help groups in eight watersheds were trained in mushroom cultivation and vermicompost preparation at the Mylampatty training centre, and 110 SHG members went on an exposure visit. In 2008–09 a further 120 farmers from Vadamadurai block visited the Nilgiris and Oddanchatram markets and Tamil Nadu Agricultural University under the scheme.

IWDP was one strand of Coodu Trust's wider watershed work: the trust's earlier published breakdown puts the complete programme at 227 watersheds in 12 districts of Tamil Nadu.

What the project delivered

  1. Community mobilization A three-tiered structure of watershed associations, executive committees, user groups and SHGs.
  2. Committee & user group training Capacity building for office bearers and members on watershed management, agriculture and group governance.
  3. SHG training & exposure visits Extensive training for SHG members, supplemented by exposure visits — 400 participants to the Horticulture Farm at Periyakulam and 70 to Rajasthan from Vedasandur block alone in 2002–03.
  4. Farm ponds & check dams Low-cost rainwater harvesting structures that conserved water and recharged groundwater.
  5. Percolation tanks Water conservation and groundwater recharge structures under holistic watershed treatment.
  6. Knowledge & documentation Training manuals for WDT members, watershed secretaries, presidents, user groups and SHGs, plus socio-economic surveys, the Shanarpatty, Vedasandur and Thogamalai project reports and mid-term evaluations.

Why this work matters

The challenge on the ground — and our answer

The challenge

Shared resources with no one to manage them

A watershed belongs to everyone and no one — without local institutions, the rural resource-poor and farmers have no organised way to govern the land and water they depend on.

Our response

Community mobilization at massive scale

A three-tiered governance structure — watershed associations and committees in 115 watersheds, with 10,270 people organised into user groups and 7,340 into self-help groups — backed by training and exposure visits.

The challenge

Rain that runs off instead of soaking in

Where rainfall is not held on the land, soil washes away, groundwater keeps falling and the productivity of whole watershed areas degrades year on year.

Our response

Structures that hold every drop

Check dams, percolation tanks and low-cost farm ponds — rainwater harvesting structures that conserved water and recharged groundwater levels across the treated watersheds.

What the project changed

Massive community mobilization

Watershed associations and committees formed in 115 watersheds, with 10,270 people organised into user groups and 7,340 into self-help groups — a wide network of community institutions.

Integrated resource management

Holistic watershed treatment on the ground — rainwater harvesting structures that conserved water and recharged groundwater levels.

Ecological balance & livelihoods

By treating entire watershed areas, IWDP worked to restore ecological balance, improve land productivity and create sustainable livelihoods for the rural resource-poor.
