Solid Waste Management Program (CSR - Renault)

Decentralised Zero-Waste Model at Padappai & Urapakkam (2022-2023)

🎯 Program Mission

Establishing a comprehensive decentralised solid waste management system as a replicable zero-waste model for peri-urban panchayats, achieving household-level segregation, eliminating open dumping and burning, and empowering sanitation workers and SHG women through scientific waste processing and recycling systems.

Programme Overview

The Solid Waste Management Program with Renault Nissan Automotive India Pvt. Ltd. (RNAIPL) was implemented as a CSR initiative at Padappai and Urapakkam peri-urban panchayats in Kanchipuram District. This project is Coodu Trust's strongest showcase case study for decentralised waste management, and the model that directly informed the larger Zero Waste Dindigul proposal to SBI Foundation.

🎯 Core Achievement

Achieved 70%+ household two-bin segregation, completely eliminated open dumping and waste burning, and operationalised the ECO-MAC system processing 2 tons of dry waste daily — establishing a replicable zero-waste model for village panchayats across Tamil Nadu.

Coodu Trust served as the on-ground implementing agency, designing and executing a comprehensive decentralised waste management system from scratch. The approach covered the entire waste value chain: household-level segregation education, door-to-door collection systems, on-site composting for organic waste, and the innovative ECO-MAC (Eco-friendly Material and Composting) system for dry waste processing.

A distinguishing feature of this programme was its strong social empowerment component. Over 20 sanitation workers were trained, equipped with safety gear, and provided dignified working conditions with additional income from recyclables. SHG women were actively integrated into the waste management value chain, and youth engagement programmes ensured community ownership and long-term sustainability.

Key Achievements & Activities

🏠

Household-Level Segregation

70%+ Compliance
3,000+ Households

Successfully introduced household-level two-bin segregation across over 3,000 households. Through sustained awareness campaigns, door-to-door education, and community engagement, the programme achieved over 70% segregation compliance — a significant achievement for a peri-urban panchayat setting.

🚛

Door-to-Door Collection System

System Established:

  • • Systematic daily collection routes
  • • Complete coverage of all households
  • • Segregated collection (wet and dry waste separately)
  • • Regular and reliable service delivery

A complete door-to-door waste collection system was established from scratch, ensuring all households in the project area had access to regular, reliable waste collection services with segregated handling of wet and dry waste streams.

🏭

ECO-MAC System

2 Tons/Day
Dry Waste Processing

The innovative ECO-MAC (Eco-friendly Material and Composting) system was operationalised, capable of processing 2 tons of dry waste daily. This system provides scientific sorting, processing, and recycling of dry waste, diverting significant volumes from landfills and creating value from waste materials.

🌱

On-Site Composting

Organic Waste Processing:

  • • Household-level composting promoted
  • • Community composting systems established
  • • Organic waste diverted from landfills
  • • Compost produced for local agricultural use

On-site composting systems were established for organic waste processing, creating a closed-loop system where organic waste is converted into useful compost rather than sent to landfills where it would generate methane emissions.

👷

Sanitation Worker Empowerment

20+ Workers
Empowered

Over 20 sanitation workers were trained, equipped with proper safety gear, and provided dignified working conditions. Workers received additional income from the sale of recyclable materials recovered through the ECO-MAC system, transforming their economic status and professional dignity.

👩‍💼

SHG & Youth Engagement

Community Integration:

  • • SHG women actively participating in waste management value chain
  • • Youth engagement programmes for community ownership
  • • Micro-enterprise potential through recyclable material processing
  • • Long-term sustainability through community ownership

Women from Self-Help Groups were integrated into the waste management system, creating livelihood opportunities through recyclable material handling and composting. Youth engagement programmes ensured broad community ownership and long-term sustainability of the zero-waste model.

Why This Model Matters

The Padappai-Urapakkam project is significant because it demonstrates that zero-waste principles can work at the panchayat level in Tamil Nadu. Key learnings that are now being scaled to the Dindigul Zero Waste Project:

🏘️ Decentralised approach works

Community-level waste processing is more effective and sustainable than centralised systems for peri-urban areas

🏠 Segregation is achievable

With sustained engagement, 70%+ household compliance is realistic even in communities with no prior waste management systems

🏭 ECO-MAC is scalable

The dry waste processing technology can be replicated across multiple locations

👥 Social empowerment drives sustainability

When sanitation workers and SHG women benefit economically, the system becomes self-sustaining

🚫 Zero-open-dumping is possible

Complete elimination of open dumping and burning is achievable within a single project cycle

This model directly informed Coodu Trust's ₹3.92 crore proposal to SBI Foundation for the "Zero Waste Dindigul" project, which aims to replicate and scale these learnings across all 48 wards of Dindigul city.
