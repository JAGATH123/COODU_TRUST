Solid waste management program (CSR – Renault)

A CSR partnership with Renault P. Ltd — putting decentralised composting machinery, village awareness work and trained sanitary crews into the Padappai and Urapakkam panchayats of Kancheepuram district.

  • CSR partnership
  • 2021–2024
  • Completed
2 Panchayats covered
4 CSR activities delivered
2021–2024 Partnership period

The project

What this project set out to do

Project mission

Putting the machinery, the awareness work and the trained crews for decentralised solid waste management into two peri-urban panchayats — so that organic waste is composted where it is generated rather than carted away and dumped.

Renault Nissan Automotive India Private Limited (RNAIPL) — named as Renault P. Ltd in the 2023–24 report — chose solid waste management as a CSR activity at Padappai Panchayat, Kancheepuram district. Coodu Trust carried it out on the ground. The core of the package was the erection of the ECOMIC composting machine at the Padappai and Urapakkam panchayats, the first piece of decentralised waste infrastructure either panchayat had.

Around the machine the trust ran a village-level solid waste awareness campaign covering social engineering, IEC, and monitoring and branding activities — and trained the panchayats’ own village sanitary workers, the sweepers, drivers and other helpers, in the maintenance and follow-up the system needs to keep running once the CSR project ends. The same partnership went on to fund daily collection and composting at Padappai, described on the Padappai Panchayat page.

  • Source segregation
  • Door-to-door collection
  • On-site composting
  • Worker & SHG empowerment

What the project delivered

  1. Erection of the ECOMIC machine The composting machine installed and commissioned at Padappai and Urapakkam panchayats, Kancheepuram district.
  2. Village-level awareness campaign A solid waste management awareness campaign run at village level, built around social engineering with the households who have to sort the waste.
  3. IEC, monitoring and branding Information, Education and Communication material, plus monitoring and branding activities that keep the system visible in the village.
  4. Training the sanitary workers The panchayats’ own sweepers, drivers and helpers trained in the maintenance and follow-up activities the machine and the collection round require.

Why this work matters

The challenge on the ground — and our answer

The challenge

No machinery, so nothing can be processed locally

A peri-urban panchayat with no composting equipment has only one option for its organic waste: send it away, or let it rot in the open.

Our response

Corporate CSR buys the missing infrastructure

Renault’s CSR funding paid for the ECOMIC composting machine to be erected at Padappai and Urapakkam — decentralised processing capacity that the panchayats then own and operate.

The challenge

Equipment without skills stops working

A waste system only lasts if the people who run it know how to run it — yet village sanitary crews are rarely trained on new machinery or on what happens after it is installed.

Our response

Train the crew, then hand the system over

The village sanitary workers — sweepers, drivers and other helpers — were trained in maintenance and follow-up, and the village-level awareness campaign, IEC and branding gave households a reason to keep sorting.

Outcomes

What the project changed

Composting capacity where there was none

An ECOMIC composting machine erected at Padappai and Urapakkam — two panchayats that can now process their own organic waste instead of sending it away.

Crews who can keep it running

The panchayats’ sweepers, drivers and helpers trained in maintenance and follow-up, so the system does not stop when the CSR project does.

A village that understands its own waste

Village-level awareness on social engineering, backed by IEC material and visible branding, so that segregation is something households do rather than something done to them.
