Rashtriya Mahila Kosh (RMK) micro-credit & livelihood project

The National Credit Fund for Women, Ministry of Women & Child Development — micro-credit and livelihood support delivered by Coodu Trust exclusively for poor women in Kadavur Block, Karur District.

  • Central government scheme
  • 2009–2015
  • Completed

The project

Credit built exclusively for poor women

Project mission

Providing micro-credit and livelihood support exclusively to poor women through the National Credit Fund for Women (Rashtriya Mahila Kosh), enabling them to build sustainable income through milch animal rearing, petty trade enterprises, and educational advancement — transforming the economic status of the most disadvantaged women in rural Kadavur Block.

The Rashtriya Mahila Kosh (RMK) is the National Credit Fund for Women — a Central Government institution under the Ministry of Women & Child Development that works exclusively for poor women. Coodu Trust was selected by RMK as an Intermediary Micro-finance Organisation (IMO) and delivered the project in Kadavur Block, Karur District, Tamil Nadu, from 2009 to 2015. In 2009–2010 the core credit activities placed 50 milch animals on loan with 50 women beneficiaries, financed 3 petty shops and extended 10 educational loans for children's schooling.

The RMK grant did not work alone. It operated alongside Mahalir Thittam (started 26.01.2006 across the Kadavur, Thogamalai, Aravakurichi and Paramathi blocks), NABARD agricultural credit linkage and SGSY/TAHDCO economic assistance schemes — a linked ecosystem that secured a ₹1.33 crore bank loan for 67 SHGs for cow rearing, established 120 commercial sheep farms and 10 goat farms with UVTRC Karur support, and trained women in dairy, tailoring, nursery, vermicomposting and mushroom production.

What the project delivered

  1. Milch animal distribution 50 milch animals on loan to fifty women in Kadavur Block (2009–2010) — daily income through milk sales plus a productive asset base.
  2. Petty shop loans Three women received loans to establish petty shops — small retail enterprises giving regular daily income as self-employed traders.
  3. Educational loans Ten loans for children's education, keeping children in school and opening higher-education opportunities.
  4. Integrated livelihood support Linked programmes: Mahalir Thittam SHG bank linkage, sheep and goat farms, backyard poultry and entrepreneurship trainings.

Why this work matters

The challenge on the ground — and our answer

The challenge

No assets, no route to daily income

Without a productive asset or start-up credit, the poorest rural women have no way to earn a regular daily income of their own.

Our response

Asset transfer that pays every day

50 milch animals on loan to 50 women brought daily income through milk sales, while 3 petty shop loans let women become self-employed traders in their own communities.

The challenge

Children's schooling cut short by poverty

When family income is precarious, children's education is often the first expense to be dropped — carrying the cycle of poverty into the next generation.

Our response

Educational loans that keep children in school

10 educational loans helped women beneficiaries keep their children in school and access higher education opportunities — investing credit directly in the next generation.
