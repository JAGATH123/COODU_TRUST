Rashtriya Mahila Kosh (RMK) micro-credit & livelihood project

The National Credit Fund for Women, Ministry of Women & Child Development — micro-credit and livelihood support delivered by Coodu Trust exclusively for poor women in Kadavur Block, Karur District.

  • Central government scheme
  • 2007–2012
  • Completed
₹7.5L RMK first-phase outlay, 2008–09
50 Milch animals on loan to 50 women, 2009–10
3 Petty shop loans
10 Educational loans

The project

Credit built exclusively for poor women

Project mission

Providing micro-credit and livelihood support exclusively to poor women through the National Credit Fund for Women (Rashtriya Mahila Kosh), enabling them to build sustainable income through milch animal rearing, petty trade enterprises, and educational advancement — transforming the economic status of the most disadvantaged women in rural Kadavur Block.

The Rashtriya Mahila Kosh (RMK) is the National Credit Fund for Women — a Central Government institution under the Ministry of Women & Child Development whose loans are available solely and entirely to poor women. Coodu Trust applied to RMK for a loan in 2007–08. By 2008–09 the trust reported itself as being supported by RMK, with a first-phase financial outlay of ₹7.5 lakh working among 40 women's self-help groups: 30 trained women farmers were each provided with one milch animal, and ten more received credit for petty shop and sheep rearing activities.

In 2009–2010 the credit activities placed 50 milch animals on loan with 50 women beneficiaries, financed 3 petty shops and extended 10 educational loans, all for beneficiaries of Kadavur Block, Karur district. Separately, under a calf-rearing scheme the same year, 40 women were trained and 38 of them availed a loan under RMK. The fund is last named in the 2011–12 report, both among the trust's supporting organizations and among the schemes to which its self-help groups were linked.

The RMK support did not work alone. It ran alongside Mahalir Thittam, under which Coodu Trust was the approved NGO for Karur district from 26.01.2006 across the Kadavur, Thogamalai, Aravakurichi and Paramathi blocks, together with NABARD credit linkage and the SGSY and TAHDCO economic assistance schemes.

What the project delivered

  1. Milch animal distribution 30 women farmers given one milch animal each in the ₹7.5 lakh first phase (2008–09), and 50 milch animals on loan to fifty women in Kadavur Block (2009–2010) — daily income through milk sales plus a productive asset base.
  2. Petty shop loans Three women received loans to establish petty shops — small retail enterprises giving regular daily income as self-employed traders.
  3. Educational loans Ten loans for children's education, keeping children in school and opening higher-education opportunities.
  4. Calf rearing training & finance 40 women beneficiaries trained in calf rearing before finance was released; 38 went on to avail a loan under the scheme.
  5. Integrated livelihood support Linked programmes: Mahalir Thittam SHG bank linkage, NABARD credit and the SGSY and TAHDCO assistance schemes.

Why this work matters

The challenge on the ground — and our answer

The challenge

No assets, no route to daily income

Without a productive asset or start-up credit, the poorest rural women have no way to earn a regular daily income of their own.

Our response

Asset transfer that pays every day

50 milch animals on loan to 50 women brought daily income through milk sales, while 3 petty shop loans let women become self-employed traders in their own communities. The reports are explicit that the animals were provided on loan, not given.

The challenge

Children's schooling cut short by poverty

When family income is precarious, children's education is often the first expense to be dropped — carrying the cycle of poverty into the next generation.

Our response

Educational loans that keep children in school

10 educational loans helped women beneficiaries keep their children in school and access higher education opportunities — investing credit directly in the next generation.

Outcomes

What the project changed

Asset transfer & daily income

50 milch animals on loan to 50 women enabled regular income through dairy farming and built a productive asset base, following 30 animals placed with trained women farmers in the first phase.

Enterprise & education

3 petty shop loans let women become self-employed traders, while 10 educational loans helped break the cycle of poverty by investing in children's education.

Training before finance

Under the calf-rearing scheme, 40 women beneficiaries were trained before any money moved, and 38 went on to take a loan — the trust's own report insists that farmers be trained well in calf rearing before finance is made available.
