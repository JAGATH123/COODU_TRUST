Programme Overview

The Rashtriya Mahila Kosh (RMK) — the National Credit Fund for Women — is a Central Government institution under the Ministry of Women and Child Development, working exclusively for poor women. RMK's loans are available solely and entirely to this target group, recognising that among the poor, women are the most disadvantaged — characterised by lack of education and access to resources required for upward economic and social mobility.

🎯 Core Rationale

Evidence shows that groups of women are better customers than men — they are better managers of resources, benefits of loans are spread wider among the household when routed through women, and the track record of all-women groups is consistently better than that of all-male or mixed groups. RMK develops the supply side of the micro-finance market by offering institution building support through structures of incentives, technology transfer, staff training, and other non-financial services.

Coodu Trust was selected as an Intermediary Micro-finance Organisation (IMO) by RMK to implement micro-credit programmes in Kadavur Block of Karur District, Tamil Nadu. Through this partnership, Coodu Trust provided milch animals on loan to women beneficiaries, enabling them to generate regular income through dairy farming. The programme also included petty shop loans for women to start small trading businesses and educational loans to support their children's education.

This RMK project operated in conjunction with Coodu Trust's broader women empowerment work through Mahalir Thittam in Karur district, which included SHG formation, financial inclusion training, and bank linkage for livestock rearing loans. Together, these complementary programmes created a holistic support ecosystem for poor women in the region.

Coodu Trust provided 50 milch animals on loan to fifty women beneficiaries in Kadavur Block of Karur District. 2009-2010

Three women beneficiaries received loans to establish petty shops — small retail trading enterprises that provide regular daily income. These micro-enterprises allowed women to become self-employed traders in their local communities, selling household necessities and building economic independence through entrepreneurship.

Ten educational loans were provided to women beneficiaries for their children's education, recognising that breaking the cycle of poverty requires investment in the next generation. These loans enabled families to keep children in school and access higher education opportunities that would otherwise be financially impossible.

About Rashtriya Mahila Kosh (RMK)

The National Credit Fund for Women (Rashtriya Mahila Kosh) was established by the Government of India under the Ministry of Women and Child Development as a dedicated institution for poor women's economic empowerment. Key principles:

  • Exclusively for Poor Women: Loans available solely to women who are the most disadvantaged and lack access to formal financial services
  • Institution Building: RMK develops the micro-finance ecosystem by supporting IMOs (Intermediary Micro-finance Organisations) like Coodu Trust
  • Technology Transfer: Provides structures of incentives, technology, staff training, and non-financial services to strengthen implementing organisations
  • Sustainable Approach: Recognises that premature disbursement can reduce effectiveness — emphasises building institutional capacity before scaling
