Program Overview

Coodu Trust established a 3-acre ethno-botanical garden at its Mayilampatty field office in Karur district to conserve medicinal plant species traditionally known to ethnic communities and provide education and training to diverse beneficiaries.

The garden serves as a living repository of traditional healing knowledge, preserving medicinal plants used in Ayurveda, Unani, and Siddha systems of medicine. The initiative combines biodiversity conservation with community education, offering training programs for farmers, school children, college students, folk healers, and women's self-help groups.

This work addresses the urgent need to document and protect India's rich heritage of medicinal plants, ensuring that traditional knowledge is passed on to future generations while promoting sustainable harvesting practices.

🌿 Garden Establishment

The 3-acre ethno-botanical garden at Mayilampatty field office serves as a conservation site for medicinal plants known to ethnic communities. The garden preserves species used in traditional healing practices and provides a controlled environment for their propagation.

🌱 Species Collection

The garden focuses on medicinal plant species traditionally used by ethnic communities in Tamil Nadu. These plants represent the biodiversity heritage of the region and include species used in Ayurveda, Unani, and Siddha systems of medicine.

India's Medicinal Plant Heritage

India possesses one of the world's richest traditions of using medicinal plants for healthcare. The country is home to approximately 2,500 species of medicinal plants that have been used by traditional healers for centuries. Of these, around 100 species serve as regular sources of medicine in the Ayurveda, Unani, and Siddha systems of traditional medicine.

This rich biodiversity is under threat from habitat loss, over-harvesting, and the erosion of traditional knowledge among younger generations. Ethno-botanical gardens like the one established by Coodu Trust play a critical role in conserving these species ex-situ (outside their natural habitats) while simultaneously educating communities about their sustainable use.

By documenting traditional knowledge from ethnic communities and providing training to farmers, students, folk healers, and women's groups, Coodu Trust ensures that this invaluable heritage is preserved for future generations while creating livelihood opportunities through sustainable medicinal plant cultivation.
