DPAP Overview

The Drought Prone Area Programme (DPAP) was a centrally sponsored scheme implemented by Coodu Trust between 2001 and 2004. As one of the foundational initiatives of the organization, its primary objective was to mitigate the adverse effects of drought and prevent desertification in some of Tamil Nadu's most vulnerable regions.

🎯 Core Strategy

Implementation of integrated watershed management to conserve natural resources, restore ecological balance, and improve the productive potential of rainfed and degraded lands.

The implementation framework was highly participatory, with Coodu Trust acting as a key coordinating agency in collaboration with the District Rural Development Agencies (DRDAs) of the state government. The program was strategically focused on drought-prone districts, including Dindigul, Tirunelveli, Sivagangai, Karur, and Salem.

A central tenet of the DPAP was empowering local communities to take ownership of their resources. This was achieved by establishing grassroots institutions like Watershed Associations, Watershed Committees, and User Groups, ensuring that all development activities were planned and executed with direct community involvement.

Major Activities & Achievements (2001–2004)

🤝

Extensive Community Mobilization

24
Watershed Associations
24
Watershed Committees

The program successfully established a robust, three-tiered community governance structure. In the 2001-2002 period alone, this included the formation of 24 Watershed Associations and 24 Watershed Committees across the project areas.

👥

Formation of Grassroots Groups

1,234
User Groups
510+
SHGs Formed

Over 1,234 User Groups and 510 Self-Help Groups (SHGs) were formed across 24 watersheds in Dindigul, Tirunelveli, and Sivagangai districts, ensuring widespread participation in project activities.

💧

Water & Soil Conservation

  • • Low-cost farm ponds for water storage
  • • Nullah bunds and check dams
  • • Percolation tanks for groundwater recharge

Key interventions successfully reduced rainwater runoff, increased water levels in wells, recharged groundwater, and curbed topsoil erosion across project areas.
