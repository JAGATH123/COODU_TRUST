Organic farming practices

Promoting sustainable, eco-friendly farming methods for healthier crops and environment.

2,345 Farmers trained, 2013–14
1,456 Rural youth trained, 2013–14
2,134 Women trained, 2021–22

Understanding

What organic farming means

What is organic farming?

Organic farming is a holistic agricultural approach that emphasizes natural processes and excludes synthetic chemicals, pesticides and fertilizers — producing nutritious food while protecting the environment for future generations.

Our organic farming practices programme empowers farmers with knowledge and techniques in natural soil fertility management, biological pest control, vermicomposting, bio-fertilizer production and sustainable crop rotation. In 2013–14 alone we ran 128 organic farming trainings for 2,345 farmers and 1,456 rural youth.

This sustainable method focuses on maintaining soil health, promoting biodiversity and preserving ecological balance. The work continues: under the Mahila Kisan Sashaktikaran Pariyojana (MKSP) in Thonthoni and Kadavur blocks of Karur district, 2,134 women were trained in organic agriculture in 2021–22.

What our training covers

  1. Natural soil fertility management Building and maintaining soil fertility through natural composting, vermicomposting and organic matter addition.
  2. Biological pest control Biological controls, companion planting and herbal pesticides instead of synthetic chemicals.
  3. Vermicomposting & bio-fertilizers Hands-on production of vermicompost and bio-fertilizers as natural alternatives to purchased synthetic inputs.
  4. Sustainable crop rotation Crop diversity and rotation practices that can be maintained indefinitely without degrading natural resources.

Why this work matters

The challenge on the ground — and our answer

The challenge

Costly synthetic inputs

Expensive synthetic fertilizers and pesticides eat into farm earnings season after season.

Our response

Natural inputs made on the farm

Training in vermicomposting and bio-fertilizer production replaces purchased chemicals with inputs farmers make themselves from farm and animal waste.

The challenge

Chemicals degrade the land

Synthetic chemicals damage the soil ecosystem and contaminate the water sources farms depend on.

Our response

Rebuild soil health naturally

Natural composting and organic matter addition enhance soil structure, fertility and water retention capacity.

The challenge

Chemical exposure harms health

Handling synthetic pesticides exposes farming families to harmful chemicals — and residues reach consumers too.

Our response

Safer farms, safer food

Biological pest control, companion planting and herbal pesticides improve farmer health and produce safer food for consumers.

The challenge

Produce with nowhere to go

Residue-free produce is worth more, but only if a farmer can reach a buyer who pays for the difference.

Our response

Harvest, pack and sell

Our trainings carry through to harvesting, packing and marketing, and pair organic methods with the producer companies we promote.

Impact

What organic farming changes

Soil health improvement

Natural composting and organic matter addition enhance soil structure, fertility and water retention capacity.

Reduced input costs

Vermicompost, vermiwash and herbal pesticides made on the farm replace synthetic fertilizers and pesticides that have to be bought in.

Residue-free produce

Organic input technologies were promoted with KVK Dindigul to produce nutrient-rich, residue-free vegetables and fruit for market.

Environmental protection

Chemical-free farming protects water sources and the soil ecosystem, and promotes biodiversity conservation.
